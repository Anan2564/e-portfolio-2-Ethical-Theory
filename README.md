Summary of the artefact:

 
The OAIC report is on the Qantas data breach case for 2025 in relation to the overseas third party contact centre. In June 2025, the employee was tricked into giving permission to log into the customer relationship management system by someone who impersonated Qantas IT. About 5.67 million customer details became exposed and these included names, email, phone numbers, date of birth and frequent flyer numbers. The OAIC undertook preliminary investigations but no contravention of the Australian Privacy Principles was discovered (Office of the Australian Information Commissioner 2026).

Justification on why I chose the artefact:

The reason for my choice of this report was my shock on learning that a major data breach does not necessarily imply a violation of the privacy law by Qantas.  No matter how organizations outsource services, they still retain certain obligations towards the protection of information provided to them.

Artefact 3 — Bozzini, 'The regimes of ethical hacking'


<img width="1003" height="665" alt="Screenshot 2026-08-12 at 1 05 29 PM" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fcc4b4e2-d8e0-4150-8ddf-84f102ed5405" />




Type: Peer-reviewed journal article (open access) Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2025.2498683 

Summary of the artefact:

Bozzini looks into the development of ethical hacking and vulnerability disclosure from the 1990s until today. Three main regimes are identified: full disclosure, responsible or coordinated disclosure, and bug bounty programs. Bozzini demonstrates that ethical hacking has become increasingly standardized, normalized, and marketized (Bozzini 2025, p. 2).

Justification on why I chose the artefact:


The reason for choosing this article is that before reading it, I used to view ethical hacking only as a cyber security profession. However, I came to know that ethical hacking has evolved through time and that different ways of ethical hacking have different ethical values.  In addition, the Week 4 anti-worm case has shown me that even though an action may be done with good intention, it can still be considered unethical based on the outcomes of the action (CQUniversity 2026).
