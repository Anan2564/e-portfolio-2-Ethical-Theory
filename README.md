# e-portfolio-2-Ethical-Theory
Ethics theories and their application to actual ICT challenges via critical thinking and ethical reasoning. 

Artefact 1 — OAIC Report into the Qantas Data Breach

Type: Government report / website Link: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-assessments-and-decisions/privacy-decisions/Investigation-inquiry-reports/report-into-preliminary-inquiries-of-qantas Date: July 2026

Summary:

 
The OAIC report is on the Qantas data breach case for 2025 in relation to the overseas third party contact centre. In June 2025, the employee was tricked into giving permission to log into the customer relationship management system by someone who impersonated Qantas IT. About 5.67 million customer details became exposed and these included names, email, phone numbers, date of birth and frequent flyer numbers. The OAIC undertook preliminary investigations but no contravention of the Australian Privacy Principles was discovered (Office of the Australian Information Commissioner 2026).


The reason for my choice of this report was my shock on learning that a major data breach does not necessarily imply a violation of the privacy law by Qantas. This helped me realize that legal and ethical responsibilities are not always the same. I believe that Social Contract Theory is applicable in this case because customers have reasonable expectations regarding their personal data being kept private. No matter how organizations outsource services, they still retain certain obligations towards the protection of information provided to them.


Artefact 2 — ABC News report on the Qantas cyber attack

Type: Video (news) Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pCOraWqBPOY Date: 2 July 2025

Summery:

The news story by ABC News on the Qantas cyber attack in 2025 highlights the possibility that data of close to six million people might be exposed in such an event. The news story sheds light on the incident and the circumstances under which it took place via a breach in the third party contact center, while placing it in the context of data breaches in Australia, such as Optus and Medibank.

This video was chosen by me as it depicts the way in which a cyber attack might affect an average individual and not just computer systems of a company. What was really disturbing for me in this particular case was the manipulation of the call center representative who became a tool to get access to the customer data. This relates to Kantianism since people are supposed to be regarded as ends in themselves and not used just to satisfy somebody else's desire. News media coverage plays its role since it helps reveal the ethical aspect of cyber attacks.


Artefact 3 — Bozzini, 'The regimes of ethical hacking'

Type: Peer-reviewed journal article (open access) Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2025.2498683 Date: 6 May 2025

Summery:

Bozzini looks into the development of ethical hacking and vulnerability disclosure from the 1990s until today. Three main regimes are identified: full disclosure, responsible or coordinated disclosure, and bug bounty programs. In the author's view, the moral projects generated by each regime are distinct in terms of their values, norms and practices in the interaction between hackers and corporations. Bozzini demonstrates that ethical hacking has become increasingly standardized, normalized, and marketized (Bozzini 2025, p. 2).

The reason for choosing this article is that before reading it, I used to view ethical hacking only as a cyber security profession which focuses on identifying weaknesses in systems. However, I came to know that ethical hacking has evolved through time and that different ways of ethical hacking have different ethical values. The full disclosure policy made me think about the positive and negative aspects of publically disclosing the weaknesses. In addition, the Week 4 anti-worm case has shown me that even though an action may be done with good intention, it can still be considered unethical based on the outcomes of the action (CQUniversity 2026).
