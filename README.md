# e-portfolio-2-Ethical-Theory
Ethics theories and their application to actual ICT challenges via critical thinking and ethical reasoning. 

Artefact 1 — OAIC Report into the Qantas Data Breach

Type: Government report / website Link: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-assessments-and-decisions/privacy-decisions/Investigation-inquiry-reports/report-into-preliminary-inquiries-of-qantas Date: July 2026

Summary:

 
The OAIC report is on the Qantas data breach case for 2025 in relation to the overseas third party contact centre. In June 2025, the employee was tricked into giving permission to log into the customer relationship management system by someone who impersonated Qantas IT. About 5.67 million customer details became exposed and these included names, email, phone numbers, date of birth and frequent flyer numbers. The OAIC undertook preliminary investigations but no contravention of the Australian Privacy Principles was discovered (Office of the Australian Information Commissioner 2026).


The reason for my choice of this report was my shock on learning that a major data breach does not necessarily imply a violation of the privacy law by Qantas. This helped me realize that legal and ethical responsibilities are not always the same. I believe that Social Contract Theory is applicable in this case because customers have reasonable expectations regarding their personal data being kept private. No matter how organizations outsource services, they still retain certain obligations towards the protection of information provided to them.
